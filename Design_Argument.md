# Design Argument — Today's Stock (오늘의 주식)

---

## Thesis

My mom is not someone who can't use AI. She just never had a tool set up for the specific task of following the stock market. Give her the right tool for the right task, and she'll use it — she's already proven that with ChatGPT at the doctor's office.

---

## The Person

My mom is a Korean housewife in her 50s. She started learning about stocks because she wants financial independence. Every day when she has time, she watches Korean stock YouTube channels, joins live streams from investment YouTubers to hear real-time market analysis, and reads the news. She knows AI is powerful. But it feels like something that younger people — her kids' generation — are good at, not her. So she sticks to what feels familiar: YouTube, news, books.

She's my mom. I've watched her spend hours studying stocks through YouTube videos. AI has made so many parts of my own life easier — research, information gathering, day-to-day tasks. I wanted her to feel that same ease. And as AI becomes more embedded in everyday life, I wanted to be the one who helps her adapt to that shift before it feels overwhelming.

---

## The Problem

The way my mom currently follows the stock market takes too much time. U.S. market news comes in English, which is hard for her to analyze on her own. There's too much information and she doesn't always know what to focus on. She knew ChatGPT existed, but didn't know how to ask it questions in a way that would give her useful answers. She understood that "good prompts" mattered but that felt like its own barrier. When she did try asking, she got answers like "investment decisions are yours to make" — and walked away with nothing. So she stopped trying.

But my mom isn't someone who can't use AI at all. She already uses ChatGPT when she goes to the doctor — asking how to explain her symptoms in English. That works because the task is clear and bounded. Stocks are different. It's an open-ended judgment problem, and she didn't know how to frame the question. The problem wasn't the person. The problem was that no one had set up the tool for this specific task.

---

## What "Helped" Looks Like

Since using Today's Stock, my mom spends less time searching across multiple YouTube videos and news sources. She can get a quick read on the market without the usual research spiral. More importantly, she's changed how she interacts with AI — she started by using it like a one-way translation service ("translate this for me"), but now she asks follow-up questions and holds back-and-forth conversations naturally. She's treating it as her tool, not someone else's.

---

## Platform Rationale — Why ChatGPT Custom GPT

My dad already has a ChatGPT Plus subscription through his company, and my mom was already using that account for hospital translations. No new app to download, no new login to figure out, no new interface to learn. A stock GPT that lives inside the app she already knows and uses. Gemini and Claude are comparable in capability, but there was no reason to move her off a tool she was already comfortable with. The platform wasn't chosen for its features. It was chosen because it's already in my mom's hands.

---

## Why Me

I use AI every day. Research, writing, organizing information — it's made a lot of things easier for me. Watching my mom study stocks through YouTube, I kept thinking: she could just ask AI to do this. But she didn't know how to ask. I did. I'm also her daughter, which means I could sit with her, watch how she actually uses it, and hear her real feedback — not a guess at what a "target user" might think. I wanted to build her something she'd actually keep using, not a demo she'd forget. And honestly, I wanted to help her feel less left behind by something that's only going to become more present in her daily life.

---

## Design Argument — One Sentence Check

> *My mom doesn't need to learn how to use AI better — she needs AI set up for the task she's already trying to do.*
